Accounts and sign-in

Ephyra uses Not Organic accounts. Signing in sends you to Not Organic, which authenticates you under its own Terms of Service and Privacy Policy. Ephyra receives your decentralized identifier (DID), your handle, and tokens that let it act within the scopes you approved.

Use accurate information and protect your sign-in and the CLI credentials file on your computer. Approve a terminal sign-in only when the code in your browser matches the one your terminal shows. Tell us at {{contact}} if you believe someone has used your account without permission.

Each deployment decides who may approve quests. Being signed in does not make you an approver. If you act for an organization, you confirm that you can bind it.

What Ephyra does on your behalf

Ephyra reads the production signals you send it and opens quests. It runs code in sandboxes and writes patches. After the approvals your autonomy policy requires, it lands changes on the forges you connect. It acts with the credentials and permissions you give the deployment, and no others.

You set the autonomy policy. It says which areas Ephyra may research or patch, and which changes need a person at checkpoints A, B and C. Approving a checkpoint is your decision to proceed. You are responsible for the changes you approve, and for anything your policy lets land without a person.

Proof, watch windows and rollback reduce risk; they do not remove it. A passing proof can miss a defect, a watch can miss a regression, and a rollback can fail or arrive late. Keep your own review, backups and deploy controls for any system where a bad change could cause harm.

Only connect repositories, forges, telemetry and systems you are authorized to read and change.
